# Team Sports Curriculum

> Maintained by `@domain-mentor`. The full L0→L10 path; each level lists skills, theory, and the assessment that unlocks the next level. Focus sports: **football (soccer) and cricket** — accessible in Bangladesh with minimal gear; basketball/volleyball introduced at L3+. This domain teaches cooperative physical play — solo conditioning lives in `domains/strength`, `domains/running`, and the training pillar; social/verbal skills off the field live in `domains/communication` and `domains/behavior`.

## L0 — Foundations
- What makes a team sport different from solo play: shared goal, roles, rules, winning AND losing together
- Meet the sports: football and cricket basics — objective, players per side, field/pitch layout, how scoring works
- Sportsmanship intro: shake hands, respect the ref, no blaming, celebrate teammates
- Assessment: explain each sport's objective + name 3 sportsmanship rules in own words

## L1 — Rules & Roles
- Football deeper: positions (keeper/defender/midfielder/striker), throw-ins/corners/fouls, match length
- Cricket deeper: batting/bowling/fielding roles, overs, outs (bowled/caught/run out), scoring runs
- Roles fit people: everyone has a job — scoring isn't the only way to matter
- Assessment: diagram a football formation + walk through a cricket innings; positions quiz 80%+

## L2 — Core Individual Skills in Team Context
- Football: dribbling basics, inside-foot pass, trapping/control, safe shooting mechanics
- Cricket: grip and stance intro, straight-bat defense, underarm/overarm throw, catching with two hands
- Skill within the team frame: control first, look up second — skills exist to serve the play
- Assessment: 10 controlled passes vs. wall/partner + 10 catches taken; demonstrate each skill's correct form

## L3 — Passing, Positioning & Spacing
- "Pass and move": the ball travels faster than any player; receiving shape and first-touch direction
- Spacing: stay spread out — don't swarm the ball; making triangles and giving options
- Cricket equivalents: calling for runs ("yes/no/wait"), backing up, fielding positions and cutting angles
- Introduce third sport flavor: basketball/volleyball spacing concepts for variety
- Assessment: small-sided keep-away game keeping shape + correctly call 10 run decisions in drills

## L4 — Communication on the Field
- The three field calls: "Man on" (pressure), "Time" (free), "Mine/keeper's" (claiming the ball)
- Cricket calling: loud, early, decisive run calls; fielders calling catches
- Body language and pointing: showing where you want the ball; talking to organize teammates
- Talking under pressure: short, loud, useful words — no noise for noise's sake
- Assessment: scrimmage where every pass/catch requires a correct call — mentor scores call accuracy 80%+

## L5 — Being a Good Teammate — Intermediate milestone
- The teammate code: effort always, blame never; everyone touches the ball; celebrate others' wins
- Resilience after errors: next-play mindset — a mistake is data, not identity; reset rituals (breath, clap, refocus)
- Supporting struggling teammates: encouragement vs. criticism; how to give a useful tip without being a coach
- Handling losing: losing well is a skill — what to do/say in the last losing minutes
- Assessment: mentor observation rubric across 3 sessions — encouragement given, blame-free errors, reset demonstrated

## L6 — Reading the Game
- Watching with purpose: where is space, who's unmarked, what is the opponent doing repeatedly
- Football: when to pass vs. dribble vs. shoot; defending as a unit (press or drop)
- Cricket: reading the bowler's plan, field placements telling you where to score, rotating strike
- Thinking ahead: your next action decided before the ball reaches you
- Assessment: narrate-live a 5-min game segment (choices + why) + decision quiz on video/diagram scenarios 80%+

## L7 — Basic Tactics
- Team plans: attacking shape vs. defending shape; playing to your side's strengths
- Football set-pieces intro: corner/free-kick roles; kickoff plays
- Cricket tactics intro: setting a field for a plan, bowling to the field, batting partnerships
- Adapting mid-game: down a goal / losing wickets — what actually changes
- Assessment: draw and explain one set-piece play + one field placement with reasoning

## L8 — Leadership on the Field
- Leading without the armband: organizing by talk, lifting heads after goals conceded, noticing who's fading
- Captaincy basics: pre-game chat, on-field decisions (bat/bowl first, subs), calm under pressure
- Managing teammates: different teammates need different words; managing an argument fast
- Assessment: lead warm-up + run a 10-min scrimmage segment as organizer; debrief rubric 80%+

## L9 — Full-Match Craft
- Putting it together across a whole match: pacing effort, staying mentally in during bench/between-overs time
- Routines: personal warm-up routine, pre-match checklist, post-match reflection habit
- Playing different roles: accept and execute whatever position the team needs today
- Assessment: play a full organized match logging reflections after each phase; self-review + mentor rubric 80%+

## L10 — Mastery — Match Organizer
- Capstone: organize and lead a real match end-to-end — rally players, agree format/rules, warm up both sides, manage substitutions, referee or appoint fairly, debrief with sportsmanship
- Teach-back: coach one beginner through a basic skill session (passing or catching)
- Community: join a regular local game / school team / academy session (guardian-approved)
- Assessment: capstone match report filed (organization + leadership rubric) + taught session + regular-game commitment logged

## Physical Rules (every level, always)
- Warm-up before every session: 5–10 min light movement + dynamic stretches; cool-down after
- Hydrate before/during/after; play through teamwork, never aggression — no dangerous tackles, throws, or contact beyond age-appropriate rules
- Injury stop-signal: pain, dizziness, head knock, or "something feels wrong" = stop immediately, tell an adult → `@safety-guardian`
- All contact scaled to age group; equipment checks (ball condition, safe surface, boundaries away from hazards)
