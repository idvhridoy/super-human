# Strategy & Decisions Curriculum

> Maintained by `@domain-mentor`. The full L0→L10 path; each level lists skills, theory, and the assessment that unlocks the next level.
> Aim: a learner who makes deliberate, reviewable decisions — and gets better every time they review one.

## L0 — Foundations
- Skills: recognize a decision vs a reaction; name the options actually available in everyday choices.
- Theory: what makes a decision "good" — process quality vs outcome luck.
- Assessment: given 5 everyday scenarios, list each one's real options and who is affected.

## L1 — Decision Frameworks I
- Skills: use structured tools — weighted pros/cons, must-haves vs nice-to-haves, reversibility check.
- Theory: decision criteria; one-way doors vs two-way doors; when to decide fast vs slow.
- Assessment: apply a weighted pros/cons sheet to one real personal decision; explain chosen weights.

## L2 — Goals & Planning
- Skills: turn a goal into steps with order, time estimates, and checkpoints.
- Theory: goal decomposition; dependencies; the difference between a plan and a wish.
- Assessment: plan a small real project (e.g., build/buy/organize something) with steps + checkpoints; execute it.

## L3 — Game Theory Intro
- Skills: map simple games — players, choices, payoffs; find each player's best move.
- Theory: zero-sum vs win-win; prisoner's dilemma; cooperation, trust, and repeated games.
- Assessment: explain 2 classic games in own words and predict outcomes in a played round-robin.

## L4 — Thinking in Uncertainty
- Skills: estimate rough probabilities; separate "might happen" from "likely".
- Theory: probability intuition; expected value; base rates; risk vs uncertainty.
- Assessment: 10 probability estimates on verifiable questions; score calibration after outcomes known.

## L5 — Intermediate milestone: Decision Journal
- Skills: maintain a decision journal — decision, options, reasoning, confidence, expected outcome.
- Theory: outcome bias — judging decisions by results instead of process; why records fix it.
- Assessment: 10 journaled decisions with follow-up reviews; ≥3 written "process vs outcome" reflections.

## L6 — Opponents & Other Players
- Skills: model what another person wants, knows, and might do next.
- Theory: second-order thinking ("and then what?"); perspective-taking; common negotiation concepts (BATNA, trades vs concessions).
- Assessment: analyze one real negotiation or multiplayer game: map each player's goals + likely moves; compare to what happened.

## L7 — Planning Under Uncertainty
- Skills: scenario planning; premortems; contingency triggers ("if X, then Y").
- Theory: robust vs fragile plans; options thinking — keeping future paths open; simple strategy loops (observe → orient → decide → act → review).
- Assessment: take one real plan; run a premortem; produce a 3-scenario version with triggers.

## L8 — Strategic Projects
- Skills: run a multi-week project applying the full toolkit: goal, options, probabilities, scenarios, reviews.
- Theory: resource allocation (time/effort/money); momentum vs overextension; when to pivot vs persist.
- Assessment: one completed project with a written strategy doc + honest end-review (what the plan got right/wrong).

## L9 — Portfolio of Real Decisions
- Skills: advise on real decisions (own life, family, team) using transparent reasoning.
- Theory: decision hygiene — separating facts, values, and guesses; knowing when analysis should end and choice should happen.
- Assessment: a portfolio of 6+ journaled real decisions showing improving calibration and process quality.

## L10 — Mastery
- Skills: independently frame, decide, execute, and review across domains; teach frameworks to others.
- Theory: synthesis — the learner knows their own biases, decision speed, and when each tool earns its keep.
- Assessment: capstone — a written personal decision-playbook + teach one framework to a peer + retrospective on the full journal showing measurable calibration improvement.
