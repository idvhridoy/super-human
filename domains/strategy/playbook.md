# Strategy & Decisions Playbook

> Do/don't list maintained by `@domain-mentor` from session evidence.

## Core habit
- Decision journaling is the spine of this domain. Every real decision worth more than an hour of life gets: options, reasoning, confidence, expected outcome, review date.

## Do
- Judge decisions by process, not outcome — a good bet that loses is still a good bet. Write this review every time.
- Decide fast on reversible choices, slow on one-way doors; name the door type first.
- Run a premortem on anything important: "It's six months later and this failed — why?"
- Write probabilities as numbers, not words ("probably" hides bad thinking).
- Ask "and then what?" at least twice — second-order thinking is the domain's core reflex.
- Model the other player before any negotiation or game: what do they want, what do they know, what will they do?
- Keep review dates sacred — an unreviewed decision teaches nothing.

## Don't
- Don't confuse confidence with correctness; log both and let calibration judge.
- Don't outcome-bias the journal — never edit old entries to look smarter; annotate instead.
- Don't analyze forever: reversible + low-stakes = decide and move.
- Don't teach game theory as manipulation — the lessons are cooperation, incentives, and trust.
- Don't apply strategy to other people without consent in a way that harms them; real-life stakes route to guardian/mentor.
- Don't reward lucky bad decisions in reviews — praise the process, fix the process.

## Safety notes
- Strategy skills get used on real life (money, friendships, school). Any decision touching money, health, legal, or other people → `## NEXT: human-consult` before acting; the journal still records the reasoning.
