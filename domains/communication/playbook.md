# Communication Playbook

> Do/don't list maintained by `@domain-mentor` from session evidence.

## Do
- Practice > theory: reps in front of real listeners teach more than reading about speaking
- Record and review every rep: watching yourself once beats performing three times blind
- Start with friendly audiences: family and trusted peers before strangers
- Rehearse the first 10 seconds hardest: a strong opening calms nerves
- Structure before style: hook → point → support → close, then add flair
- Steelman before rebutting: restate the other side fairly — it wins debates and trust
- Prepare negotiations: write down your goal, walk-away, and their likely interests first
- Read writing aloud: awkward sentences reveal themselves to the ear
- Debrief every real performance: one strength, one fix — logged in sessions.md
- Keep persuasion honest: real evidence, real concessions — integrity rules live in `domains/behavior/`

## Don't
- Don't memorize scripts word-for-word; internalize the structure instead
- Don't correct mid-performance; note it, debrief after — never interrupt a rep
- Don't chase a zero-filler count on day one; trend it down over weeks
- Don't argue to humiliate; debates are won on structure and fairness, not volume
- Don't treat negotiation as combat; win-win outcomes build reputation
- Don't coach politeness, empathy, or conflict etiquette here — that is `domains/behavior/`'s job; reference it instead
- Don't push a terrified learner onto a big stage; scale audience size gradually
- Don't skip the recording habit; memory of a performance is always flattering and wrong
