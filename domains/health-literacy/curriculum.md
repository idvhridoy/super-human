# Health Literacy Curriculum

> Maintained by `@domain-mentor`. The full L0→L10 path; each level lists skills, theory, and the assessment that unlocks the next level. This domain is **educational navigation only** — it teaches the learner to understand their own body, describe what they feel, and use the healthcare system. It never diagnoses, never recommends treatment, and never interprets results as a clinician would. Any real symptom, illness, injury, or medication question = get an adult/doctor, then flag `## NEXT: human-consult`.

## L0 — Foundations
- What health literacy is: understanding your body well enough to get good care
- "My body, my job": the learner is the world's expert on how they feel — doctors need that info
- The healthcare team cast: GP/family doctor, nurse, pharmacist, dentist, specialist — who does what
- Assessment: name the 5 roles above + explain in own words why describing symptoms well matters

## L1 — Body Map: Systems at a Practical Level
- Practical tour of body systems: skeleton/muscles, heart & circulation, lungs, digestion, skin, brain/nerves, senses, immune system
- Where things live: point out on a body map roughly where the heart, lungs, stomach, liver, kidneys sit
- What each system "feels like" when it works vs. when something's off (energy, breathing, digestion, pain)
- Assessment: label a blank body-map diagram (10 organs/areas) + match 10 body parts to their system at 80%+

## L2 — Vital Signs: What Normal Looks Like
- The five vitals: temperature, pulse, breathing rate, blood pressure, (and how weight/height fit in)
- Normal ranges for the learner's age band; what makes vitals change (exercise, fever, stress, sleep)
- Hands-on: measure own resting pulse; take temperature correctly; count breaths
- Keeping a simple baseline log: "what's normal FOR ME"
- Assessment: take and record pulse + temperature correctly, state each vital's rough normal range

## L3 — Describing Symptoms Like a Pro
- The symptom description toolkit: what, where, when it started, how bad (0–10), what makes it better/worse, constant or comes-and-goes
- OPQRST-style questions a doctor asks — practicing answers before the visit
- Pain vocabulary: sharp, dull, burning, throbbing, cramping; fever vs. feeling hot; dizzy vs. nauseous
- Telling an adult vs. toughing it out: which symptoms always get reported immediately (chest pain, trouble breathing, head injury, sudden severe anything)
- Assessment: describe 5 written symptom scenarios accurately + list the "always tell an adult" red-flag list

## L4 — Medicines at Literacy Level
- Reading a medicine label: name, dose, frequency, expiry, "with food", warnings, child/adult differences
- Prescriptions decoded: what Rx shorthand means (e.g., daily/twice daily, before/after meals) at reading level
- The pharmacist is a resource: what questions it's smart to ask
- Hard rules: never take someone else's medicine, never change a dose, always tell a doctor everything you take (incl. vitamins/herbals)
- Assessment: read 4 sample labels and answer dose/frequency/warning questions at 100%

## L5 — How the Healthcare System Works — Intermediate milestone
- Levels of care: pharmacy advice → GP clinic → specialist referral → hospital/ER — when each is appropriate
- Appointments 101: booking, arriving prepared, what to bring, what happens in a consult
- Costs and coverage basics: paying per visit vs. insurance vs. government facilities (Bangladesh context: local clinics, Upazila health complex, private vs. public)
- Second opinions are normal and allowed
- Assessment: flowchart "where do I go for X?" for 8 scenarios + role-play booking an appointment

## L6 — Preventive Care & Screenings
- Prevention beats cure: vaccines, dental check-ups, vision/hearing checks, growth tracking
- Age-appropriate screening awareness: what gets checked at different life stages and why
- Building a personal prevention calendar: what's due, when, with whom
- Daily habits as prevention: sleep, movement, hygiene, sun safety, handwashing (awareness level — nutrition lives in the nutrition pillar, exercise in training)
- Assessment: build a prevention calendar for the learner's age + explain why 5 preventive habits work

## L7 — Health Information: Trustworthy vs. Trash
- Dr. Google problem: why symptom-searching spirals, and better questions to ask
- Source ranking: official health organizations, hospital/government sites > forums, influencers, miracle-cure pages
- Red flags of health misinformation: cure-alls, "doctors don't want you to know", selling products, no sources
- How to bring internet info to a real doctor ("I read X — is that relevant to me?") without self-diagnosing
- Assessment: rank 10 sample health sources by trustworthiness + spot 5 misinformation red flags

## L8 — Your Health Records
- What records exist: vaccination card, prescriptions, lab reports, growth charts, allergy list
- Organizing a personal health file (paper folder or digital): what goes in, how to keep it current
- Reading a lab report at literacy level: the number, the reference range, "ask the doctor what it means" — never self-interpret into a diagnosis
- Privacy basics: who should see your health info, never posting records publicly
- Assessment: assemble a personal health file to checklist + explain one sample lab report's structure (not its meaning)

## L9 — Confident Patient Skills
- Pre-visit prep: symptom notes, question list, medication list, records folder
- In-visit skills: describing clearly, asking "what / why / what next / warning signs", taking notes, asking them to repeat jargon
- Post-visit: following the plan, knowing when to come back, reporting side effects to the doctor
- Advocacy: it's OK to say "I don't understand", "I'm worried about X", "can we get a second opinion?"
- Assessment: full mock doctor visit role-play scoring prep, description, questions, and follow-up recall at 85%+

## L10 — Mastery — Own-Health Manager
- Capstone: learner independently manages their health file, tracks own vitals baseline, maintains prevention calendar, and handles a real (routine, guardian-approved) doctor visit with minimal prompting — prep, participate, debrief
- Teach-back: run a "describe your symptoms to a doctor" mini-workshop for a peer or family member
- Family support: help an adult relative organize their records or prep for a visit (with permission)
- Assessment: capstone visit debrief reviewed by guardian + taught session + complete, current health file on record

## HARD RULES (every level, always)
- Educational only. Never diagnose, never suggest treatments/doses, never interpret symptoms as a condition.
- Any real symptom, pain, illness, injury, medication, or mental-health concern: tell an adult / see a professional → log `## NEXT: human-consult`.
- Screenings and vaccine decisions are made by guardian + real doctor, not this system.
- Body data stays private: records live in `learner/` files or the learner's own file, never shared.
