# Astronomy Playbook

> Do/don't list maintained by `@domain-mentor` from session evidence.

## SAFETY RULE #1 — THE SUN
- NEVER look at the Sun with naked eyes, binoculars, or a telescope without a proper, purpose-made solar filter covering the front aperture — instant, permanent eye damage is real and painless
- Never use sunglasses, smoked glass, film, or eyepiece-end "solar filters" — they are unsafe
- Pointing an unfiltered telescope/binoculars at the sun can also destroy the instrument; keep the cap on during daytime transport

## Do
- Check cloud forecast, moon phase, and tonight's visible planets BEFORE planning a session — work with the sky, not against it
- Let eyes dark-adapt 20–30 minutes; use a red flashlight or red mode on screens only
- Start naked-eye, then binoculars, then telescope — each step multiplies what you can find
- Dress warmer than the forecast suggests; bring water, and observe from a safe, known spot
- Keep an observing log: date, time, conditions, instrument, objects, sketch — even failed hunts count
- Use averted vision (look slightly beside the target) for faint objects
- Coordinate night sessions with `@sleep-guardian` / the sleep window: set a hard end time, plan late sessions before weekends or low-cost mornings, never trade mandatory sleep minimums for sky time — check `pillars/sleep/` before scheduling past the usual bedtime
- Tell someone where you're observing and when you'll be back; minors need an adult present at night

## Don't
- Don't point any optical instrument at or near the sun — see Safety Rule #1
- Don't use white light at an observing site (headlamps, phone screens on full white) — it wrecks night vision for everyone
- Don't trespass or set up alone in unsafe/unknown locations at night
- Don't chase faint targets under a bright moon or heavy light pollution — pick moonless windows
- Don't buy equipment before L5 concepts are understood and a human approves the purchase
- Don't promise an event will be visible — weather, timing, and latitude decide; always have a plan-B target

## Dark-sky etiquette
- Arrive before dark; shield all lights; ask before shining anything near other observers
- Keep noise low; don't touch others' equipment without permission
- Leave the site cleaner than found; respect local rules for parks/rooftops

## Safety notes
- Night sessions happen in the dark: trip hazards, insects, stray animals, cold — scout the site in daylight first
- Weather aborts are normal; log them anyway and reschedule, don't push a session into unsafe conditions
- `@safety-guardian` may append vetoes, especially for late-night sessions conflicting with sleep minimums
