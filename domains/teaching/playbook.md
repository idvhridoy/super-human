# Teaching Playbook

> Do/don't list maintained by `@domain-mentor` from session evidence.

## Do
- Diagnose the learner's model first: find out what they already think before explaining anything
- Explain-it-back daily: teaching starts with knowing whether *you* understand — Feynman gaps reveal it
- Chunk ruthlessly: 2–3 pieces per explanation; more is a lecture, less is a hint
- One strong analogy beats five weak ones; map the familiar onto the new
- Show before telling: demonstrate the skill first, then break it into steps
- Check understanding with questions, not head-nods: "tell me what you got" beats "got it?"
- Have the learner perform: teach-back and independent tries are the only real proof
- Let them struggle a little: hands off, mouth on — guide, never do it for them
- Re-teach differently when a check fails: new analogy, smaller step, fresh example — never just louder
- Log every real teach: what was taught, who learned it, did the teach-back pass

## Don't
- Don't lecture without checks: uninterrupted explanation hides total misunderstanding
- Don't explain what you don't understand yourself — go re-learn it first (Feynman rule)
- Don't use jargon to teach jargon; define or drop every hard term
- Don't accept "yeah I get it" as evidence; only a restate or performance counts
- Don't correct harshly mid-try; note it, let the attempt finish, debrief after
- Don't do the task for the learner to save time — it saves nothing, it skips the learning
- Don't shame wrong answers; a surfaced misconception is a win — it shows where to teach
- Don't plan lessons that end without a check; the check IS the lesson's point
- Don't teach advanced steps before prerequisites are proven; sequence beats speed
- Don't measure success by how smooth you sounded; measure what the learner can now do
