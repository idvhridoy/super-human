# Teaching Curriculum

> Maintained by `@domain-mentor`. The full L0→L10 path from explain-it-back to mentoring mastery; each level lists skills, theory, and the assessment that unlocks the next level. Scope: explaining, simplifying, example-making, checking understanding, tutoring, lesson design, and mentoring. The learner only ever teaches what they genuinely know — this domain teaches *how* to teach, not the subject matter itself. All content filtered by learner age (`learner/profile.md`).

## L0 — Foundations
- Explain-it-back (Feynman): restate something just learned in your own words, no notes
- Gap detection: notice where your own explanation stalls — that is the gap to revisit
- Plain words: say it like you would to a friend, not like a textbook
- **Unlock:** explains one recently learned concept back clearly enough that a listener can restate the main point

## L1
- Simplifying ideas: strip jargon, define any term that must stay
- Analogy basics: "it works like ..." — one familiar thing mapped onto the new idea
- Chunking: break a big idea into 2–3 digestible pieces
- **Unlock:** takes a school/textbook paragraph and re-explains it in half the words without losing the core meaning

## L2
- Making examples: generate a concrete example for an abstract idea
- Worked examples: show one full solved case before asking anyone to try
- Counterexamples: show what the idea is *not* — boundaries make concepts stick
- **Unlock:** produces an original example + one counterexample for a taught concept; a listener identifies which is which

## L3
- Checking understanding: ask instead of assume — "tell me what you got from that"
- Teach-back: have the learner explain it back to you; their version reveals their model
- Diagnosing misconceptions: find *where* their model breaks, not just that it's wrong
- **Unlock:** teaches a small concept with at least two checks; identifies and fixes one real misunderstanding on the spot

## L4
- Teaching a peer a real skill: demonstrate → guided try → independent try
- Step sizing: small enough steps that the learner succeeds at each one
- Patience norms: let them do it — hands off, mouth on; no doing it for them
- **Unlock:** teaches a peer one real physical or digital skill (tie, fold, build, a game move, a software action) until the peer performs it alone

## L5 — Intermediate milestone
- Designing a mini-lesson: objective, explanation, practice, check — written before delivery
- Objective clarity: one sentence — "by the end they can ___"
- Timing: plan realistic minutes per part; end with the check, not a rush
- **Unlock:** writes and delivers a 10–15 minute mini-lesson plan; the learner passes the end-check

## L6
- Lesson delivery: pacing, voice, writing while talking, managing attention drift
- Question craft: open questions that test thinking, not yes/no that tests luck
- Adapting mid-lesson: re-explain differently when the check fails — analogy switch, slower steps, new example
- **Unlock:** delivers a mini-lesson where a mid-lesson check fails and the learner successfully re-teaches the point a different way

## L7
- Tutoring: one-on-one multi-session help on a real subject the learner knows well
- Error-first practice: give problems that surface the misconception, then address it
- Feedback skill: specific, kind, actionable — "your second step skipped the carry" not "wrong"
- **Unlock:** tutors one peer across 3+ sessions on a real topic; the peer's independent performance measurably improves

## L8
- Mentoring: guiding growth over weeks, not just transferring facts
- Scaffolding and fading: support early, remove support as competence grows
- Motivation care: protect confidence while correcting — the learner must want to come back
- **Unlock:** mentors a younger learner or peer through a multi-week goal; maintains a shared progress log; the mentee reaches the goal

## L9
- Curriculum thinking: sequence a topic over multiple sessions — prerequisites first, hardest at peak energy
- Assessment design: checks that test understanding, not memorized phrasing
- Material creation: write your own examples, exercises, and review cards for a mentee
- **Unlock:** designs a 4+ session mini-curriculum for a real learner; delivers it; adjusts the plan based on check results

## L10 — Mastery
- Teaches anything they know: unfamiliar audience, limited prep, still lands
- Develops independence: mentees learn how to learn, not just what to know
- Teaches teachers: coaches another learner on teaching technique itself
- **Unlock:** capstone — a complete mini-course (designed, delivered, assessed) taught to a real learner over weeks, plus one peer coached in teaching skill, all with evidence logged
