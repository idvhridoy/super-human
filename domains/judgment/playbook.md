# Judgment Playbook

> Do/don't list maintained by `@domain-mentor` from session evidence.

## Do
- Write the decision down BEFORE deciding — options, evidence, confidence, what would flip you
- Separate verified facts from claims from unknowns — most bad calls come from treating a claim as a fact
- Ask "what would make me wrong?" on every important call — the answer is the evidence to chase
- Classify first: reversible vs irreversible, high vs low stakes — match effort to the quadrant
- Slow down hard on irreversible + high-stakes; decide fast and cheaply on reversible ones
- Record confidence as a number — "60%" can be scored later; "pretty sure" cannot
- Review journal entries at their review dates — calibration only happens with feedback
- Invite dissent on big calls — someone arguing the other side is a resource, not a threat
- Update verdicts when evidence changes and log the update — revision is the skill working
- Escalate when out of depth — money, health, legal, safety calls → `## NEXT: human-consult` or a qualified human

## Don't
- Never decide blindly — no verdict without at least naming the evidence held and the gap
- Don't confuse a good outcome with a good decision — judge the process, not the luck
- Don't rationalize backwards — the journal exists so memory can't rewrite the reasons
- Don't decide important things angry, exhausted, or rushed — state beats logic; defer when possible
- Don't fake confidence — stated honest uncertainty beats performed certainty every time
- Don't cherry-pick toward the verdict you already want — that's advocacy, not judgment
- Don't adjudicate disputes where you're a stakeholder without flagging the conflict of interest
- Don't treat mental models as verdicts — they're lenses; the decision is still yours
- Don't let "trusted judge" become "never wrong" — a public update on a bad call builds more trust than quiet correctness
