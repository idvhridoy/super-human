# Judgment Curriculum

> Maintained by `@domain-mentor`. The full L0→L10 path; each level lists skills, theory, and the assessment that unlocks the next level. Scope: the skill of DECIDING WELL — evidence standards, weighing facts vs claims, mental models, decision journals, calibration, adjudicating contested questions. Frame: the learner becomes the person whose verdicts are trusted. `critical-thinking/` owns logic, fallacies, and evidence-quality theory — applied here, not re-taught; `mind-hacks/` owns internal cognitive tactics; `mathematics/` owns probability mechanics. References them — never repeats them.

## L0 — Foundations: Decide, Don't Guess
- Skills: distinguish a decision from a guess, a wish, or an impulse; state the actual decision in one sentence.
- Theory: every decision = choice + stakes + uncertainty; "I don't know yet" is a legitimate position; never decide blindly — a bad process can get lucky once, only a good process repeats.
- Assessment: sort 10 real prompts into needs-a-decision / needs-information / just-a-preference; state each real decision in one sentence.

## L1 — Evidence Standards
- Skills: demand evidence before deciding; name what evidence would change the verdict.
- Theory: claims need support proportional to stakes; absence of evidence vs evidence of absence; what would flip you is the map of what to look for.
- Assessment: 5 decision files (own life + mini-cases) — each lists evidence held, evidence missing, and what would flip the verdict.

## L2 — Weighing Facts vs Claims
- Skills: split a decision file into verified facts, unverified claims, and unknowns; weight each item by reliability AND relevance.
- Theory: not all evidence is equal — source, freshness, directness, motive; a fact's weight = how much it matters × how sure it is.
- Assessment: 3 decision files correctly separated into facts / claims / unknowns; weighting justified in one line per item.

## L3 — Mental Models I
- Skills: apply inversion (what would guarantee failure?), second-order thinking (and then what?), and opportunity cost (what am I giving up?).
- Theory: models are lenses, not answers — each reveals what a single perspective hides; knowing a model's name ≠ using it.
- Assessment: 3 worked decisions analyzed through all three models — each model must surface something the others missed.

## L4 — Probabilistic Judgment
- Skills: attach honest confidence (%) to outcomes; compute simple expected value; think in ranges, not certainties.
- Theory: the future is a distribution, not a point; base rates anchor estimates; number-work delegated to `mathematics/` and `critical-thinking/` L7 — application here.
- Assessment: 10 scenario judgments with stated % confidence and expected-value reasoning, spot-checked by mentor.

## L5 — Intermediate milestone: The Decision Journal
- Skills: run a structured decision process end-to-end; maintain a journal — decision, options, evidence, confidence, expected outcome, review date.
- Theory: a written decision can't be retro-fitted by memory — the journal is the training instrument; judge process over outcome (a good call can lose, a bad call can win).
- Assessment: 10 journal entries over ≥2 weeks, each carrying a reviewable prediction; format defended before `@domain-mentor`.

## L6 — Calibration
- Skills: score journal predictions against outcomes; identify own over/underconfidence pattern; adjust.
- Theory: calibration = stated confidence matching hit-rate; hindsight bias rewrites memory — the journal prevents it; feedback loops are the only teacher.
- Assessment: 20+ scored predictions; calibration curve drawn; own bias pattern named with evidence.

## L7 — Adjudicating Contested Questions
- Skills: hear two or more competing cases fairly; render a verdict with reasoning; state what evidence would have changed it.
- Theory: the judge's skill is structured fairness — same standard applied to both sides; a verdict ≠ certainty ("on the evidence, X, ~70%"); steelman mechanics borrowed from `critical-thinking/` L8.
- Assessment: 3 adjudication exercises (family dispute, product choice, contested claim) — each side's case restated to its holder's satisfaction before verdict; written verdict with reasoning.

## L8 — High-Stakes Decision Frameworks
- Skills: classify decisions by reversibility and stakes; run pre-mortem and asymmetric-risk analysis; apply worst-case-bounded thinking.
- Theory: irreversible + high-stakes = slow down, gather, consult; reversible = decide fast, learn cheap; the dangerous quadrant is high-stakes + irreversible + hurried.
- Assessment: correctly triages 10 mixed decisions (decide-now / gather-more / escalate); one full pre-mortem on a real upcoming choice.

## L9 — Judgment Under Pressure & Missing Information
- Skills: decide well on 60% of the facts; use checklists and stopping rules; know when to defer, delegate, or escalate.
- Theory: paralysis is also a decision; a stopping rule ("if no new info by X, choose Y") beats endless gathering; invite dissent — someone arguing against is a resource.
- Assessment: 5 time-boxed decisions (≤10 min each) with documented stopping rules; at least one correctly deferred or escalated — graded on process, not luck.

## L10 — Mastery: High-Stakes Decision Audit (capstone)
- Skills: run the full pipeline on a genuinely consequential decision — evidence audit, model pass, probabilities, pre-mortem, verdict, journal, outcome review.
- Theory: synthesis; the trusted judge shows their work — the audit document IS the product others can rely on.
- Assessment: documented audit of one real high-stakes decision (own life or provided case): evidence table, model pass, confidence %, verdict, outcome-vs-prediction review. Mentor-scored ≥90% on process completeness; presented to a human reviewer.
