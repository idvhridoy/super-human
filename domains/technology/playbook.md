# Technology Playbook

> Do/don't list maintained by `@domain-mentor` from session evidence.

## Do
- Explain in plain words first, jargon second — understanding means being able to say it simply
- Trace every device through input → process → output before adding detail
- Ask "what problem did this solve?" for every technology studied
- Keep the hype filter on: marketing words (revolutionary, game-changing, AI-powered) trigger the "show me the evidence" reflex
- Evaluate before adopting: cost + data + attention + lock-in, not just features
- Cross-reference `coding/` for software detail and `engineering/` for physical builds — point there, don't re-teach
- Use teach-back constantly — explaining a device to someone else is the real test

## Don't
- Don't open, disassemble, or probe mains-powered devices — teardown is conceptual or battery-powered items only, with human approval
- Don't create accounts, install apps, or buy anything "for a lesson" — real-world transactions need human approval
- Don't treat specs as truth — benchmark claims are marketing until verified
- Don't collect gadgets to seem advanced — the goal is judgment, not consumption
- Don't repeat `digital-literacy/` safety content — assume it, link to it
- Don't believe "smart" means secure — every connected device is also a data collector
- Don't let AI hype erase the honest answer "we don't fully know how it does that"

## Safety notes (if physical)
- Physical exploration limited to battery-powered, non-mains devices; lithium batteries are never punctured, opened, or charged unattended
- Anything plugged into a wall outlet is look-don't-touch; wiring/mains work is adult-only
- Stop-signals: heat, smoke, smell, swollen battery → move away, tell a human immediately
- `@safety-guardian` may append vetoes
