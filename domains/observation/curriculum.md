# Observation Curriculum

> Maintained by `@domain-mentor`. The full L0→L10 path; each level lists skills, theory, and the assessment that unlocks the next level. Scope: the perception skill — seeing and hearing what is actually there. Eyes and ears open, never blind. `situational-mastery/` owns handling live situations — this domain feeds it raw perception; `mind-hacks/` owns internal attention tactics; `digital-literacy/` owns online noticing. References them — never repeats them. All drills age-appropriate per `learner/profile.md`; people-observation is strictly respectful — never surveillance, following, or recording (see playbook red lines).

## L0 — Foundations: Looking vs Seeing
- Skills: explain the difference between glancing and observing; run the describe-3-things habit (leave a place, name 3 details noticed).
- Theory: attention is a spotlight — you see what you aim it at; most people move through scenes on autopilot; observation is a trainable habit, not a talent.
- Assessment: describe-3-things logged daily for a week; explains inattentional blindness (the selective-attention experiment) in own words.

## L1 — Noticing Drills
- Skills: score on structured noticing drills — Kim's game (memory tray), spot-the-difference, detail-hunt photos.
- Theory: memory and noticing train together; deliberate encoding ("say it in your head") beats hoping to remember.
- Assessment: 80%+ on a 15-item memory-tray test after 60-second viewing; 5 spot-the-difference sets completed.

## L2 — Environmental Scanning
- Skills: build the entrance-scan habit — on entering any space: exits, people count, layout, hazards, mood.
- Theory: baseline first — what is normal for THIS place; scanning is a 5-second relaxed loop, not a paranoid stare.
- Assessment: 10 entrance scans logged (exits / people / mood / hazard); a companion confirms the habit is invisible to others.

## L3 — Detail Reading
- Skills: read objects and scenes for story — wear patterns, placement, what's missing, what changed.
- Theory: details are evidence of process — a worn handle, fresh paint, an item out of place; inference must stay labeled inference, never presented as fact.
- Assessment: 5 scene-reads (photo or real) — each: 3 observations + 1 labeled inference + 1 stated uncertainty.

## L4 — Reading People (Respectfully)
- Skills: notice body-language clusters — posture, orientation, pace, mood — for people you interact with and groups in shared space.
- Theory: baseline the person before reading change; clusters beat single cues; body language suggests state, never proves intent — mind-reading is fiction.
- Assessment: 10 people-reads logged on consented/family or own group settings; each names the cue cluster AND the limit of the read.

## L5 — Intermediate milestone: Scene Recall
- Skills: reconstruct a scene after leaving it — structured recall protocol (people, layout, exits, sequence, anomalies).
- Theory: recall is retrieval practice — the attempt itself builds the skill; gaps in recall reveal where attention wasn't aimed.
- Assessment: 5 recall write-ups scored against photos/reality by mentor — ≥70% detail accuracy, gaps identified and named.

## L6 — Scanning in Motion
- Skills: maintain layered awareness while moving — walking routes, transit, shops; track own position and the changing scene.
- Theory: moving scenes need rhythmic re-scans (every ~30s or on change); head-up posture and unoccupied ears are the enablers.
- Assessment: 5 logged moving-route observations (route, what was noticed, what changed en route); zero phone-during-walk violations.

## L7 — Anomaly Detection
- Skills: spot what doesn't fit — the out-of-place object, the off-pattern behavior, the change from last time.
- Theory: anomalies are only visible against a learned baseline; most anomalies are benign — notice, verify, don't alarm; detection lives here, response lives in `situational-mastery/`.
- Assessment: anomaly log — 10 entries (what was off, baseline it broke, how verified, outcome); ≥6 confirmed benign — no false-alarm spirals.

## L8 — Sustained & Divided Attention
- Skills: hold awareness over long periods (events, waits, travel) without fatigue collapse; split attention across channels safely.
- Theory: attention fatigues — plan micro-recovery cycles; vigilance degrades after ~20–30 min without variation; the skill is cycling, not white-knuckling.
- Assessment: 3 sustained-observation exercises (≥30 min) with accuracy checks at start / middle / end — demonstrate the cycle technique.

## L9 — Layered Observation
- Skills: run simultaneous layers — scene + people + changes + own state — in complex environments (events, markets, busy transit).
- Theory: layers run on different clock speeds; the observer's own state (tired, stressed, distracted) is the first thing to monitor — a compromised observer reads a compromised scene.
- Assessment: 3 layered-observation write-ups from real complex environments — 4 layers each, self-state logged, mentor-scored ≥80%.

## L10 — Mastery: The 360-Degree Observer (capstone)
- Skills: continuous natural awareness — nothing forced; notices first, recalls accurately, flags anomalies calmly, teaches it.
- Theory: synthesis; mastery is awareness that looks like relaxation — always aware, never on edge.
- Assessment: capstone week — 7 days logged with daily scan + recall + anomaly entries; one live field test (mentor places changes in a scene, learner finds ≥80%); teach describe-3-things + entrance scan to a peer or younger learner.
