# Music Curriculum

> Maintained by `@domain-mentor`. The full L0→L10 path; each level lists skills, theory, and the assessment that unlocks the next level.
> Framing rule for every level: ears first, then hands, then notation. Short consistent practice beats rare long sessions — always.

## L0 — Foundations: Ears & Rhythm
- Skills: clap steady beats; echo short rhythms; sing back short melodies roughly in tune; tell high vs low and loud vs soft.
- Theory: what music is made of — beat, rhythm, melody, dynamics; listening as an active skill.
- Assessment: keep a steady beat through 2 songs; echo 5 four-beat rhythms accurately; a 1-week listening log (3 songs/day, one line each on what was noticed).

## L1 — Instrument Basics
- Skills: correct posture and hand position on the chosen instrument; produce a clean first sound; play first notes/a first scale fragment.
- Theory: how the chosen instrument makes sound; care and handling; note names on the instrument.
- Assessment: hold and play 5 clean notes with correct posture for 3 consecutive sessions.

## L2 — First Songs
- Skills: play 3 simple songs/patterns start to finish at slow tempo without stopping.
- Theory: playing along with a beat; basic counting (1-2-3-4); slow practice as the master technique.
- Assessment: record or perform 3 short pieces; tempo steady enough to tap along.

## L3 — Reading Notation
- Skills: read treble (or instrument-appropriate) staff: note names, basic note values (whole, half, quarter), rests.
- Theory: the staff, clef, measures/bar lines, time signature basics; matching written note → instrument position.
- Assessment: sight-read 5 short (4-bar) exercises at slow tempo with correct notes and rhythm.

## L4 — Scales, Chords & Technique
- Skills: play 2–3 major scales (or equivalent patterns) cleanly; basic chord shapes or hand patterns per instrument.
- Theory: what a scale is and why keys exist; chords as stacked notes; tempo control with a metronome.
- Assessment: play 2 scales hands-clean at a set metronome tempo; explain what key a piece is in.

## L5 — Intermediate milestone: First Performance
- Skills: prepare and perform one complete piece for a live audience (family counts) from memory or score.
- Theory: combining L0–L4; practice structure — warm-up, slow work, run-through; basic stage habits (start calm, recover from slips).
- Assessment: one full piece performed for an audience + a written reflection on what happened and what to fix.

## L6 — Repertoire & Expression
- Skills: build a repertoire of 5+ pieces; play with dynamics (loud/soft shaping) and articulation.
- Theory: expression markings; phrasing — music as sentences; style differences between genres the learner likes.
- Assessment: perform 2 contrasting pieces demonstrating clear dynamics and phrasing choices.

## L7 — Music Theory
- Skills: analyze a piece: key, chord functions, form (sections), and one interesting detail.
- Theory: intervals, chord construction, basic harmony (I–IV–V and friends), song form (verse/chorus/AABA), how tension and release work.
- Assessment: written analysis of 2 favorite songs — key, chords, structure, and why one moment "works".

## L8 — Performance Craft
- Skills: perform regularly; manage nerves; recover from mistakes mid-performance; learn a piece to performance standard in ≤3 weeks.
- Theory: deliberate practice vs run-throughs; mental rehearsal; performance anxiety and breathing; tempo integrity under pressure.
- Assessment: 3 logged mini-performances with self-recordings; one written "mistake recovery" analysis.

## L9 — Composition & Improvisation
- Skills: improvise over a simple chord progression; compose an original 8–16 bar piece and write/record it.
- Theory: melody construction — motifs, repetition, variation; matching melody to chords; simple arrangement.
- Assessment: one original composition (recorded + notated or charted) presented with a composer note explaining the choices.

## L10 — Mastery: Musician Capstone
- Skills: function as a rounded musician — perform, read, analyze, create — at self-directed level.
- Theory: synthesis of all levels; developing a personal musical taste and practice system that sustains itself.
- Assessment: capstone recital — 3 prepared pieces spanning styles + 1 original composition, performed for an audience, plus a program note written by the learner.
