# Music Playbook

> Do/don't list maintained by `@domain-mentor` from session evidence.

## Core framing (hard rule)
- **Consistent short practice beats rare long sessions — always.** 15–20 minutes daily outperforms a weekend marathon. Guard the streak; never punish a missed day with a punitive double session.
- Ears first, hands second, notation third — a learner who can hear and feel the music reads it faster later.

## Do
- Practice slow enough to be correct; speed is a *result* of accuracy, not an input.
- Split every session: warm-up / hard slow work / fun piece — end on something enjoyable.
- Use a metronome for technique work; record performances and listen back — the recording never flatters.
- Isolate hard bars: loop 2–4 measures instead of restarting the whole piece.
- Keep the listening log alive at every level — musicians grow by hearing more than they can play.
- Celebrate small performances early and often (family audience counts); performance is a skill trained separately from practice.
- Stop or reduce on pain; hands and voice are load-bearing — treat discomfort as a flag, not a weakness.

## Don't
- Don't grind full run-throughs of a piece with known broken spots — fix the bars, then re-integrate.
- Don't practice while frustrated past usefulness — a 5-minute reset beats 20 sloppy minutes.
- Don't skip rest days in heavy technique phases; fatigue consolidates errors.
- Don't compare repertoire or speed to other learners or online performers — compare to last month's recording.
- Don't let notation-reading delay playing; learn songs by ear alongside reading work.
- Don't chase volume on the instrument for hours; shorter focused reps with rests protect hands and voice.

## Safety notes
- Physical: watch for hand/wrist strain, sore throat (voice), hearing exposure (volume limits on headphones and amplified practice). Pain or persistent discomfort → stop and flag `## NEXT: human-consult` for guardian review.
- Instrument purchase, lessons, or event sign-ups are guardian-approved decisions only.
