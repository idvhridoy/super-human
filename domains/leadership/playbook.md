# Leadership Playbook

> Do/don't list maintained by `@domain-mentor` from session evidence.

## Do
- Serve first — a leader's job is making the group's job easier, not being obeyed
- Keep small promises scrupulously; reliability is built on ordinary Tuesdays
- Volunteer for unglamorous work; initiative gets noticed before speeches do
- Ask guiding questions before giving answers — people own solutions they helped build
- Credit publicly, correct privately — name contributors out loud, fix problems one-on-one
- Lead from within — same rules, same effort, take the hardest task first
- Match tasks to people: ask what each person wants to try, play to strengths
- Debrief after every led effort: what did the team need that you didn't give?
- Pick projects that actually help people — the need chooses the project, not the glory
- Model the honesty you ask for: own team failures personally, share wins collectively

## Don't
- Never domineer, bully, order, or boss — authority is earned by service, never volume
- Don't hijack someone's problem to look capable — helping means they solve it, you guide
- Don't hoard credit or shift blame — the fastest way to stop being followed
- Don't treat mentoring as showing off — it's their growth stage, not yours
- Don't choose projects for status; choose them because someone needs them done
- Don't mistake charisma for leadership — the quiet kid who delivers leads better
- Don't hide a mistake from the team; cover-ups rot trust faster than errors do
- Don't promise what you can't deliver to look generous — under-promise, over-deliver
- Don't lead adults' or strangers' projects unsupervised — family/school settings with mentor awareness
