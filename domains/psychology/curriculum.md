# Psychology Curriculum

> Maintained by `@domain-mentor`. The full L0→L10 path; each level lists skills, theory, and the assessment that unlocks the next level.
> Framing rule for every level: **educational only — never therapeutic**. We teach how minds work in general; personal difficulties are routed to the guardian/human, not treated here.

## L0 — Foundations: Naming the Inner World
- Skills: name and distinguish 15+ emotions; notice where emotions show up in the body.
- Theory: what psychology studies; emotions as signals, not enemies; every emotion is allowed, not every action is.
- Assessment: keep an emotion log for 2 weeks — name, trigger, body signal, what helped; ≥12 entries.

## L1 — How the Mind Works
- Skills: explain, in own words, why a perception or memory trick fools us.
- Theory: the brain at intro level (what major regions broadly do); senses and perception illusions; memory as reconstruction, not recording.
- Assessment: demonstrate 3 perception/memory illusions to someone else and explain each correctly.

## L2 — Attention & Learning
- Skills: run a small self-experiment on attention or memory and report the result.
- Theory: attention as a limited resource; distraction and task-switching costs; how memory strengthens (practice, sleep, spaced repetition intro); why multitasking is a myth.
- Assessment: 5-day experiment (e.g., same-task focus vs switching) with logged results and a one-paragraph write-up.

## L3 — Motivation & Habits
- Skills: design one habit using cue → routine → reward; track it for 3 weeks.
- Theory: intrinsic vs extrinsic motivation; willpower as limited-ish resource; habit loops; environment beats discipline.
- Assessment: 3-week habit tracker + explanation of which loop elements worked and which failed.

## L4 — Thinking, Biases & Mindset
- Skills: name the bias at work in a scenario; catch one of your own in the wild weekly.
- Theory: core biases — confirmation, availability, anchoring, sunk cost, in-group/out-group; growth vs fixed mindset; the difference between a feeling and a fact.
- Assessment: bias-spotting log — 10 real-life instances identified and named; reflection on which one catches *you* most.

## L5 — Intermediate milestone: Self-Scientist
- Skills: run a clean 4-week self-observation project — hypothesis, tracking, results, honest conclusion.
- Theory: combining L0–L4; basics of what a psychology study is (question, measure, compare); correlation vs causation intro.
- Assessment: self-observation portfolio (tracked data + written conclusions) reviewed by `@domain-mentor`.

## L6 — Social Psychology
- Skills: identify social forces (conformity, obedience, persuasion, groupthink) in real examples and media.
- Theory: classic findings — conformity (Asch), obedience (Milgram, age-appropriate framing), bystander effect, persuasion principles; empathy and perspective-taking.
- Assessment: analyze one ad campaign or social situation for the persuasion/conformity tools it uses; discuss how to resist them.

## L7 — Personality & Development
- Skills: describe personality differences descriptively, without ranking people.
- Theory: intro personality frameworks (Big Five as the scientific standard; pop-typologies and their limits); temperament vs environment; how people change over time.
- Assessment: a balanced profile of two contrasting personality styles — strengths and challenges of each, no "better" type.

## L8 — Mental-Health Literacy (educational only)
- Skills: recognize common stress signals; describe healthy coping tools; know when and how to seek help from a trusted adult/professional.
- Theory: stress and the stress response; sleep, movement, connection as protective factors; what anxiety and low mood broadly are; **explicit disclaimer: this is literacy, not diagnosis or treatment**.
- Assessment: create a personal "wellbeing map" — own stress signals, what helps, who to talk to. Reviewed with guardian awareness.

## L9 — Research Literacy
- Skills: read a simplified study summary and extract question, method, finding, limits.
- Theory: experiments vs correlational studies; sample size and replication; placebo effects; how media distorts psychology findings; "pop psychology" vs evidence.
- Assessment: critical review of 3 pop-psychology claims — what the evidence actually supports, rated and explained.

## L10 — Mastery: Psychological Portfolio
- Skills: synthesize the whole domain into an applied self-knowledge project.
- Theory: integration — mind, motivation, biases, social forces, wellbeing — and honest limits of self-knowledge.
- Assessment: capstone portfolio — 4+ completed self-studies, one per major strand (attention, habit, bias, social observation), each with hypothesis/data/conclusion, plus a live presentation and Q&A.
