# Mobility & Recovery Playbook

> Do/don't list maintained by `@domain-mentor` from session evidence.

## Do
- Practice daily — 10 consistent minutes beats an occasional long session.
- Warm the body first: mobility after waking needs extra-gentle entry; a few minutes of movement or post-training is ideal.
- Breathe slowly (nasal, long exhales) through every stretch — breath-holding fights the stretch.
- Work both sides equally; note asymmetries in `metrics.md`.
- Move slowly into range; hold 20–30 s per position; back off to a comfortable version.
- Anchor the routine to a fixed daily cue (morning, after school, before bed) so the streak survives.
- Use mobility as recovery on rest days for running/strength.

## Don't
- No ballistic bouncing or forcing range — especially when cold.
- Never push into sharp, stabbing, or pinching pain; mild stretch discomfort (2–3/10) is the ceiling.
- No partner-forced stretches — nobody else pushes the learner deeper.
- Don't skip days to "save up" flexibility — range decays fast without frequency.
- No loaded end-range work before L7 and never past a controlled, light load.
- Don't copy extreme flexibility content (contortion, deep backbends) — out of scope.

## Safety notes (if physical)
- Stop signals: sharp/joint pain, numbness or tingling, dizziness, pain that lingers after the session.
- Normal stretch sensation = mild pulling that eases with breathing; anything worsening = back off or stop.
- Age-appropriate limits: minors' joints are naturally more mobile — the emphasis is control and quality, not maximum range. No aggressive flexibility training (oversplits, forced backbends) for minors — growth-plate and joint-capsule protection.
- Spine rules: loaded spinal flexion (e.g., Jefferson curls) only at L7+, light, slow, and never with pain.
- Any persistent joint pain, suspected hypermobility issues, or pain flags → `@safety-guardian` + human consult.
- `@safety-guardian` may append vetoes.
