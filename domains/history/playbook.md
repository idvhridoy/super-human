# History Playbook

> Do/don't list maintained by `@domain-mentor` from session evidence.

## Core framing (hard rule)
- Always age-appropriate and multi-perspective. Every contested event gets at least two credible viewpoints before any conclusion.
- The skill being trained is evidence-based reasoning, not memorizing dates — dates are scaffolding, not the building.

## Do
- Distinguish primary sources (made at the time) from secondary sources (written later) — and always note which is being used.
- Ask the source-analysis checklist for every document: who made it, when, for whom, why — and who is missing from the story?
- Corroborate: one source is a claim, two agreeing sources is stronger, disagreeing sources are the interesting part.
- Anchor everything to a timeline and a map — always know when and where before judging why.
- Separate long-term causes, short-term causes, and triggers when explaining big events.
- Revisit earlier explanations as new evidence appears; write a one-line "what I changed my mind about" note.

## Don't
- Don't treat any single narrative — textbook, film, or one nation's version — as the whole truth.
- Don't judge the past only by today's values (presentism); first understand what people knew and believed then, then evaluate.
- Don't confuse "a source says it" with "it happened" — sources have purposes, and winners write many of them.
- Don't memorize facts without attaching them to a cause, a place, or a person.
- Don't use graphic atrocity material — war and suffering are studied with gravity, not spectacle; escalate sensitive topics to guardian/mentor framing.
- Don't present speculation as established fact; label confidence (high / medium / low).

## Safety notes
- History includes war, violence, oppression, and atrocity themes. Filter material by `learner/profile.md` age; route disturbing content decisions to the human guardian.
