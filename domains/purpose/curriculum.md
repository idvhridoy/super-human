# Purpose Curriculum

> Maintained by `@domain-mentor`. The full L0→L10 path; each level lists skills, theory, and the assessment that unlocks the next level.
> Framing rule: ikigai + meaning — exploratory, never preachy. The learner discovers their purpose; the mentor asks questions and runs experiments, never assigns an answer. Syncs continuously with `learner/goals.md` and `@career-mentor` — purpose is the compass, not the cage. Revisited quarterly; "I don't know yet" is always a valid state.

## L0 — Foundations: Values Inventory
- Skills: name what actually matters — sort, rank, and discuss personal values (honesty, freedom, family, mastery, adventure, service...); distinguish stated values from lived values.
- Theory: values are what you'd protect even when it costs you; everyone has them, few have examined them; noticing the gap between what you say and what you do is the starting data.
- Assessment: values card-sort completed and discussed — top 5 values named with one real example each of the value in action (or conspicuously missing).

## L1 — Strengths Mapping
- Skills: inventory what you're actually good at — with evidence, not wishes; gather external observations from people who know you.
- Theory: strengths are repeatable patterns of strong performance + energy; self-assessment alone lies — triangulate with evidence from `learner/assessments.md`, domain progress files, and feedback from others.
- Assessment: strengths map — 5+ strengths, each backed by one piece of real evidence (result, project, someone else's observation).

## L2 — Interests & Curiosity Mapping
- Skills: track what absorbs you — activities where time disappears; maintain a curiosity log fed by `learner/interests.md`; distinguish deep interests from passing novelty.
- Theory: attention is honest even when words aren't — what you return to voluntarily is data; interests evolve; the map is a snapshot, not a sentence.
- Assessment: 2-week curiosity log reviewed — 3 recurring interest patterns identified and named in learner's own words.

## L3 — The Ikigai Framework
- Skills: place strengths, interests, observed needs, and realistic pay paths onto the four circles — love / good at / needed / paid; read the overlaps (passion, mission, vocation, profession).
- Theory: ikigai is a lens, not a verdict — overlaps suggest directions to test, not destinies; each circle can be grown deliberately; "paid" is honest data about the world, not a sellout.
- Assessment: first ikigai diagram completed — each circle filled with real entries from L0–L2 evidence, overlaps discussed with `@domain-mentor`.

## L4 — Meaning & Contribution
- Skills: identify who benefits from your work and care — past, present, possible; run small contribution experiments (help, build, teach, fix) and log what felt meaningful vs empty.
- Theory: meaning is found in contribution more reliably than in consumption or achievement; meaning ≠ pleasure — some meaningful work is hard and boring on the day; purpose research consistently points to "useful to others" as the durable source.
- Assessment: contribution experiment log — 3 small acts of contribution tried, each logged with how it actually felt (energized / neutral / drained) and why.

## L5 — Intermediate milestone: Drafting a Personal Mission
- Skills: write a first-draft personal mission statement — short, honest, testable; connect it to current `learner/goals.md` entries.
- Theory: a mission statement is a hypothesis to test in the world, not a poem to admire; good drafts name who you serve, how, and why it matters to you; it will be revised many times — drafting beats perfecting.
- Assessment: mission draft v1 written and reviewed — stated in ≤3 sentences, links to ≥2 live goals in `learner/goals.md`, flagged as draft.

## L6 — Testing the Mission
- Skills: run small real-world experiments that probe the draft mission — projects, roles, volunteering-style tasks, electives from `learner/interests.md`; capture what confirmed and what surprised.
- Theory: purpose is tested by contact with reality, not by thinking harder; cheap experiments beat big commitments; a failed test is information, not failure of the mission.
- Assessment: 2+ mission-test experiments completed and debriefed — what confirmed, what needs revision; draft updated or deliberately kept with reasons.

## L7 — Living-Goal Alignment Reviews
- Skills: run a quarterly alignment review — do current goals, schedule, and daily habits reflect stated values and mission; find and name drift honestly.
- Theory: drift is normal — goals ossify, life changes, values sharpen; alignment reviews are maintenance, not confession; the review's output is a small set of goal edits in `learner/goals.md`, synced with `@career-mentor` for career-relevant items.
- Assessment: first full alignment review completed — goals scored keep/adjust/drop against values + mission, edits logged in `learner/goals.md`.

## L8 — Purpose Under Change
- Skills: revise values, strengths, and mission as the learner grows — distinguish evolution from abandonment; handle the "my old purpose feels wrong now" moment without panic.
- Theory: purposes grow like the person — the teenager's map shouldn't be the adult's cage; a purpose you consciously revise is stronger than one you never question; losing a purpose isn't losing yourself — it's updating a map.
- Assessment: written revision exercise — one element of values/mission deliberately re-examined, kept-or-changed with reasoning; "what changed and why" narrative in own words.

## L9 — Holding Purpose Alongside Others
- Skills: articulate the framework to a peer — help someone else do a values sort or curiosity log without prescribing answers; listen for borrowed purposes (parental dreams, peer pressure, status scripts) in self and others.
- Theory: explaining the framework proves mastery of it; helping others find their purpose is contribution practice, not recruitment; borrowed purposes feel like purpose until they're tested — the tell is "for whom am I doing this?"
- Assessment: one peer guided through a values or interests exercise (mentor-observed); written reflection on one borrowed-purpose risk honestly examined in self.

## L10 — Mastery: The Purpose Charter — Capstone
- Skills: author a living purpose charter — values, strengths, mission, contribution commitments, review rhythm — as the learner's own document, in the learner's own words.
- Theory: the charter is the capstone artifact and the working tool — purpose made explicit, revisable, and load-bearing for real decisions; mastery here means the learner can run the whole process alone: inventory → map → draft → test → review → revise.
- Assessment: capstone — purpose charter written and ratified by the learner: values ranked, mission stated, contribution commitments named, quarterly review rhythm scheduled, explicit links to `learner/goals.md`; defended in discussion with `@domain-mentor` as honest (not aspirational fiction).
