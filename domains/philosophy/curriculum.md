# Philosophy Curriculum

> Maintained by `@domain-mentor`. The full L0→L10 path; each level lists skills, theory, and the assessment that unlocks the next level.
> Framing rule for every level: age-appropriate, argument-first. We train clear thinking and intellectual honesty — never what conclusion to reach.

## L0 — Foundations
- Skills: ask and record "big questions"; distinguish a question with an answer from a question to think about.
- Theory: what philosophy is — wondering systematically; question types (factual vs conceptual vs ethical); the idea that reasons matter.
- Assessment: keep a question journal for 2 weeks (≥10 entries); pick one and explain two possible answers with reasons.

## L1 — Claims & Reasons
- Skills: identify the claim someone is making and the reasons given for it; tell an argument apart from an opinion, a story, or a command.
- Theory: premises and conclusions; what makes a reason relevant; "because" as the engine of reasoning.
- Assessment: dissect 5 everyday arguments (ads, rules, debates) into claim + reasons; flag the weakest reason in each.

## L2 — Logic Basics
- Skills: test arguments for validity; spot the 6–8 most common fallacies in conversation and media.
- Theory: valid vs sound; deduction vs induction; core fallacies — ad hominem, straw man, false dilemma, slippery slope, appeal to popularity, correlation ≠ cause.
- Assessment: fallacy hunt — find and correctly name 10 real examples; fix 3 broken arguments so they become valid.

## L3 — Ethics I: Right & Wrong
- Skills: apply three lenses to a dilemma — consequences, rules/duties, character ("what would a good person do").
- Theory: introduction to consequentialism, deontology, and virtue ethics through stories and dilemmas (trolley-style problems age-adapted); fairness, promises, harm.
- Assessment: analyze one dilemma through all three lenses; state a position and name the strongest objection to it.

## L4 — Major Thinkers Survey
- Skills: summarize a thinker's core idea accurately before criticizing it.
- Theory: guided tour — Socrates and the examined life; Plato's forms and the cave; Aristotle on virtue and purpose; Confucius, the Buddha, and Laozi (Eastern strands); a taste of later figures (Descartes, Kant, Mill, Arendt) per age fit.
- Assessment: "thinker cards" — 8 one-page profiles (idea, best argument for it, one criticism); explain two aloud.

## L5 — Intermediate milestone: Reasoned Position Writer
- Skills: write a structured position essay: claim → reasons → objection → reply → conclusion.
- Theory: combining L1–L4; steel-manning — stating the opposing view so well its defenders would agree it's their view.
- Assessment: 2-page position essay on a self-chosen question that includes the strongest objection, steel-manned, and a reply. Mentor-reviewed.

## L6 — Epistemology: What Can We Know?
- Skills: evaluate a knowledge claim's support — perception, testimony, reasoning, memory.
- Theory: belief vs knowledge; justified true belief and its problems; skepticism and Descartes' doubt; perception illusions; sources of error.
- Assessment: audit 5 of your own beliefs — where each came from, how confident you should be, what would change your mind.

## L7 — Mind, Self & Identity
- Skills: argue positions on mind-body questions using defined terms.
- Theory: what is a mind; consciousness as a puzzle; personal identity over time (ship-of-Theseus style problems); free will vs determinism at intro level.
- Assessment: short paper defending a position on one mind/identity puzzle, engaging one counter-argument.

## L8 — Society & Applied Ethics
- Skills: map a real-world debate onto its underlying principles.
- Theory: justice and fairness (Rawls-style veil of ignorance simplified); rights; freedom and its limits; applied areas — technology ethics, animal ethics, lying and honesty case studies.
- Assessment: take one live real-world issue; write the principled case for two opposing sides, then your own verdict.

## L9 — Deep Dive: One Thinker or One Problem
- Skills: sustained engagement with a single primary text or single hard problem over multiple weeks.
- Theory: reading philosophy closely — reconstructing arguments charitably, noting context, tracking interpretations that disagree.
- Assessment: 6–8 page study: the thinker/problem, the argument reconstructed fairly, the strongest criticism, your own position.

## L10 — Mastery: Original Philosophy
- Skills: produce an original, rigorous, honest piece of philosophical writing defending your own reasoned position.
- Theory: synthesis of all levels; intellectual humility — knowing the limits of your argument; revising under criticism.
- Assessment: capstone essay (8–10 pages) on a self-chosen question, defended in live Socratic dialogue with `@domain-mentor`; must contain at least one point where you concede ground.
