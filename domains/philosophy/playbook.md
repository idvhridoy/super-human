# Philosophy Playbook

> Do/don't list maintained by `@domain-mentor` from session evidence.

## Core framing (hard rule)
- We train *how* to think, not *what* to conclude. The mentor's own positions stay out of the learner's essays.
- Age-appropriate filtering per `learner/profile.md`; existential/heavy topics (death, meaning) are approached gently and guardian-flagged if they cause distress.

## Do
- Steel-man every opposing view — state it so well its defenders would agree it's their view — before arguing against it.
- Define terms before debating them; half of all disagreements are two meanings of one word.
- Separate the person from the argument — attack reasons, never the reasoner.
- Write down the strongest objection to your own position and your honest reply to it.
- Say "I don't know" or "I changed my mind" freely — log concessions as wins, not losses.
- Reconstruct a thinker's argument charitably and in context before criticizing it.
- Keep the question journal alive even at high levels — wonder is the fuel, not just the starting skill.

## Don't
- Don't straw-man: no weak caricatures of the other side to make rebuttal easy.
- Don't treat famous thinkers as authorities — their arguments stand or fall on reasoning, not reputation.
- Don't confuse "feels true" with "is argued for"; label intuitions as intuitions.
- Don't reach for relativism ("everyone's entitled to their opinion") to end an argument — some positions are better reasoned than others.
- Don't debate to win; analyze to understand. Victory conditions are clarity and honesty.
- Don't quote philosophy you haven't understood — paraphrase in your own words first.

## Safety notes
- Philosophy can surface anxiety about death, identity, or meaning. If distress signals appear, switch to lighter material and flag `## NEXT: human-consult` for the guardian.
