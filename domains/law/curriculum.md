# General Law Curriculum

> Maintained by `@domain-mentor`. The full L0→L10 path; each level lists skills, theory, and the assessment that unlocks the next level.
> Note: this domain builds legal literacy — it is education, never legal advice (see playbook).

## L0 — Foundations
- Skills: distinguish rules at home/school vs laws of a country.
- Theory: why societies have rules; rights and duties as two sides of every rule; fairness basics.
- Assessment: list 5 rights and 5 matching duties in everyday life; explain what happens when rules break down.

## L1 — Rights & Duties
- Skills: identify rights in realistic scenarios (school, family, online).
- Theory: basic human/child rights concepts; equality before the law; where rights come from (constitutions, charters).
- Assessment: scenario quiz — given 10 situations, name the right at stake and the corresponding duty.

## L2 — How Laws Are Made
- Skills: trace an idea from proposal to enforceable law in the learner's own country.
- Theory: legislatures, executives, courts; constitutions; national vs local law; who enforces.
- Assessment: flowchart a real (simplified) bill's path; name the branches and each one's job.

## L3 — Courts & Disputes
- Skills: explain how a dispute moves through a court; roles of judge, lawyers, jury, witnesses.
- Theory: civil vs criminal cases; burden of proof; appeals; alternatives to court (mediation, arbitration).
- Assessment: sort 8 case descriptions into civil/criminal; explain "innocent until proven guilty".

## L4 — Contracts
- Skills: spot offer, acceptance, and consideration in everyday agreements.
- Theory: what makes an agreement binding; written vs verbal; capacity (why minors' contracts differ); fair vs unfair terms.
- Assessment: diagram 3 real-world agreements (purchase, subscription, promise) labeling the contract elements — or why none exist.

## L5 — Intermediate milestone: Everyday Lawyer
- Skills: apply L1–L4 to daily life — purchases, school rules, online accounts, borrowing.
- Theory: property basics (ownership, borrowing, theft vs accident); introduction to criminal vs civil consequences.
- Assessment: pass a 20-question "life legal literacy" test at 80%+ covering rights, contracts, property, and courts.

## L6 — Criminal & Property Basics
- Skills: define key concepts (intent, evidence, proportionality); read a simplified penal summary.
- Theory: elements of a crime; defenses in principle; property/ownership, inheritance, lost-and-found rules at concept level.
- Assessment: explain what must be proven for a given (age-appropriate, hypothetical) offense; distinguish accident/negligence/intent.

## L7 — Legal Literacy
- Skills: read short real legal texts — a statute section, a terms-of-service clause, a constitution article.
- Theory: how laws are written and interpreted; definitions sections; ambiguity and interpretation principles.
- Assessment: summarize 3 real legal clauses in plain language without losing meaning.

## L8 — Reading Cases
- Skills: brief a case — facts, issue, rule, holding, reasoning (FIRAC/IRAC-lite).
- Theory: precedent; majority vs dissent; how judges reason; why similar cases matter.
- Assessment: write 2 case briefs from provided simplified landmark decisions.

## L9 — Applied Analysis
- Skills: analyze novel situations using rights + contracts + criminal + property reasoning.
- Theory: digital life (privacy, data, intellectual property basics); consumer and tenant rights at concept level.
- Assessment: 3 written "opinion memos" on fictional disputes, each citing the relevant principle and counter-argument.

## L10 — Mastery
- Skills: independent legal literacy — read any everyday legal document and explain it accurately.
- Theory: synthesis; knowing when a question needs a real licensed professional.
- Assessment: capstone — brief one real (public, age-appropriate) judgment + a 20-question comprehensive exam at 85%+; articulate the limits of self-help and when to consult a lawyer.
