# Critical Thinking Curriculum

> Maintained by `@domain-mentor`. The full L0→L10 path; each level lists skills, theory, and the assessment that unlocks the next level.
> Framing rule for every level: applied to **own beliefs first**. `psychology/` explains what biases are, `digital-literacy/` covers online misinformation mechanics, `mathematics/` grounds the probability — this domain builds the reasoning skill that uses them all.

## L0 — Foundations: Claim, Reason, Feeling
- Skills: tell a claim from a reason from a feeling; answer "why do you think that?" about own opinions.
- Theory: opinions vs supported claims; every conclusion sits on reasons (good or bad); feelings are real but aren't evidence.
- Assessment: sort 20 mixed statements into claim / reason / feeling at 90%+; give a reason for 5 own opinions unprompted.

## L1 — Argument Structure
- Skills: find the main claim and supporting premises in short texts; draw a simple argument map.
- Theory: premise → conclusion structure; signal words (because, therefore, so); hidden assumptions; an argument can fail by bad premises OR bad logic.
- Assessment: map 5 real arguments (ads, articles, conversations) — claim, premises, one hidden assumption each.

## L2 — Deduction vs Induction
- Skills: distinguish "must be true" from "probably true"; evaluate simple syllogisms.
- Theory: deductive validity vs soundness; inductive strength; why one counterexample kills a universal claim; certainty is rare — most real reasoning is about degrees.
- Assessment: 10-item exercise sorting valid/sound/weak arguments with one-line justifications.

## L3 — Fallacies I: The Usual Suspects
- Skills: name and explain ad hominem, straw man, false dilemma, slippery slope, appeal to authority — in examples, then in the wild.
- Theory: a fallacy is a pattern that *feels* like a reason but isn't; each one has a tell; knowing names ≠ immunity — spotting in real time is the skill.
- Assessment: fallacy-spotting log — 10 real instances found and named (media, ads, conversations), each with the tell explained.

## L4 — Fallacies II + Rhetoric
- Skills: detect cherry-picking, post hoc, appeal to emotion, loaded language, anecdote-as-data; separate a claim from its packaging.
- Theory: emotion is the delivery vehicle for weak arguments; statistics without context deceive; a true anecdote can still be bad evidence; loud ≠ right.
- Assessment: dissect one emotionally charged article/ad — stripped to bare claims, then re-evaluated.

## L5 — Intermediate milestone: Full Argument Analysis
- Skills: analyze a complete real-world argument end-to-end — structure, fallacies, evidence, verdict.
- Theory: integration of L0–L4; the output is a judgment *with stated confidence*, not just criticism.
- Assessment: written analysis of one real debate/article: argument map + named fallacies + verdict ("claim supported / weakly supported / unsupported") defended before `@domain-mentor`.

## L6 — Evidence Quality & Source Evaluation
- Skills: grade evidence sources by type and motive; spot correlation-vs-causation errors and bad statistics.
- Theory: primary vs secondary sources; expertise must match the claim; sample size and selection; who benefits if I believe this?; "studies show" means nothing without which studies.
- Assessment: evidence audit — for 5 real claims, rate each source strong/medium/weak with reasons; at least one verdict changed after checking.

## L7 — Probabilistic Thinking
- Skills: use probability language honestly (likely/unlikely, %); apply base rates; update a belief when new evidence arrives; rough Fermi estimation.
- Theory: most truths are probable, not certain; base rates beat vivid stories; updating on evidence is the core skill — being wrong on purpose, early and cheaply.
- Assessment: calibration game — 20 predictions with confidence %, scored later; show at least one belief updated when evidence changed.

## L8 — Steelmanning & Fair-Mindedness
- Skills: state the strongest version of a position you disagree with — well enough that its holder agrees it's fair; hold two opposing cases open at once.
- Theory: you haven't earned disagreement until you can win the other side's argument; principle of charity; changing your mind is a success state, not a defeat.
- Assessment: 3 steelman exercises on real disagreements — partner confirms each was fair; one written case where the exercise changed or softened own view.

## L9 — Epistemic Hygiene
- Skills: run a personal information diet deliberately; notice own certainty vs actual knowledge; detect propaganda patterns at scale (repetition, manufactured consensus, emotional flooding).
- Theory: knowing what you don't know is most of wisdom; minds get colonized through repetition and tribe, not argument; intellectual humility is structural, not personality.
- Assessment: 2-week info-diet audit + "certainty map" — 10 things believed, each rated by actual evidence quality; flag the weakest-held strong beliefs.

## L10 — Mastery: Applied BS-Detection Capstone
- Skills: deploy the full toolkit live on real-world claims across domains — ads, news, viral posts, sales pitches, own long-held beliefs.
- Theory: synthesis — structure, fallacies, evidence, probability, steelmanning, hygiene; the final skill is doing it fairly, fast, and on yourself first.
- Assessment: capstone portfolio — 8+ claim audits across ≥4 domains (each: claim, analysis, verdict, confidence), one self-audit of a cherished belief, plus a live BS-detection demonstration scored by `@domain-mentor` at 90%+.
