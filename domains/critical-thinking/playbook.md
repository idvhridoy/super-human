# Critical Thinking Playbook

> Do/don't list maintained by `@domain-mentor` from session evidence.

## Do
- Apply the tools to your own beliefs first — self-audit before auditing anyone else's argument
- Always state confidence with a verdict — "supported, ~70%" beats a bare yes/no
- Ask "who benefits if I believe this?" for every persuasive message
- Steelman before you disagree — earn the disagreement first
- Keep a changed-my-mind log — updates are trophies, not losses
- Rate the evidence, not the speaker — but remember expertise must match the claim
- Sit with "I don't know" comfortably — it's a legitimate terminal answer
- Check base rates before trusting vivid stories or headlines

## Don't
- Don't use fallacy names as conversation weapons — the goal is clarity, not point-scoring or humiliating people
- Don't let skepticism curdle into cynicism — "everything is fake" is lazy thinking wearing a smart costume
- Don't cherry-pick evidence while accusing others of cherry-picking
- Don't confuse loud, confident, or repeated with true — repetition is a delivery mechanism, not evidence
- Don't treat "studies show" as a conclusion — which studies, how big, measuring what, funded by whom?
- Don't pattern-match to reach a desired verdict — run the same analysis you'd run on a claim you hate
- Don't perform certainty — honest probability language (likely, ~60%, I don't know) is the discipline
- Avoid arguing against someone's identity or tribe in exercises — analyze claims, not people
