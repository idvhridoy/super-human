# Geopolitics Curriculum

> Maintained by `@domain-mentor`. The full L0→L10 path; each level lists skills, theory, and the assessment that unlocks the next level.
> Framing rule for every level: age-appropriate, non-partisan, multiple-perspective. We study how power works, not which side is "right".

## L0 — Foundations
- Skills: world map literacy — continents, oceans, 30+ countries by shape and location.
- Theory: what a country/state is; government vs country vs nation; borders and why they exist.
- Assessment: label a blank world map (30/40 correct); explain "state", "nation", "government" in own words.

## L1 — Power & Resources
- Skills: read thematic maps (population, resources, trade routes).
- Theory: what makes a state powerful — geography, population, economy, military, technology.
- Assessment: compare two countries across 5 power factors in a one-page table.

## L2 — How States Interact
- Skills: track one ongoing international story for a week using 3+ different sources.
- Theory: allies, rivals, treaties, trade, diplomacy, sanctions — the basic toolbox of state behavior.
- Assessment: explain one real international dispute naming each side's stated interest.

## L3 — Regions of the World
- Skills: regional map mastery; identify each region's key players and fault lines.
- Theory: region-by-region survey — South Asia, East Asia, Middle East, Europe, Africa, the Americas, Arctic; each region's geography → interests → tensions.
- Assessment: write 3 regional profiles (1 page each: players, resources, main rivalry, main partnership).

## L4 — Institutions & Rules
- Skills: diagram how a decision moves through a major institution.
- Theory: UN, Security Council, ICC, WTO, NATO, regional blocs (ASEAN, AU, EU, OIC), treaty law basics; what institutions can and cannot enforce.
- Assessment: explain one institution's purpose, members, and one real case where it acted (or failed to).

## L5 — Intermediate milestone: Country Analyst
- Skills: produce a structured country brief (geography → economy → military → allies/rivals → outlook).
- Theory: combining L1–L4 into a repeatable analysis template.
- Assessment: publish 3 country briefs reviewed by `@domain-mentor`; peer-explain one aloud.

## L6 — Analysis Frameworks
- Skills: apply the same event through at least two competing lenses.
- Theory: major schools — realism, liberalism, constructivism — plus geostrategic tools (chokepoints, buffer states, spheres of influence, balance of power); each presented as a lens, not a truth.
- Assessment: analyze one current event twice — once through realism, once through liberalism — and state which fits better and why.

## L7 — Economics of Power
- Skills: read trade-flow and energy maps; explain one sanction regime end to end.
- Theory: sanctions, trade wars, reserve currencies, energy dependence, supply chains, debt diplomacy.
- Assessment: trace one commodity (oil, chips, grain) from producer to consumer and name 3 leverage points.

## L8 — Scenarios & Forecasting
- Skills: build base/alternative scenarios; assign rough probabilities; keep a forecast log.
- Theory: drivers vs outcomes, black swans, escalation ladders, scenario planning method.
- Assessment: 5 written forecasts with probabilities + stated assumptions; review calibration after 60 days.

## L9 — Regional Deep-Dive
- Skills: produce an original multi-week study of one region using primary + diverse secondary sources.
- Theory: historical roots of current alignments; how domestic politics shapes foreign policy.
- Assessment: 8–10 page regional assessment with timeline, actor map, and 2 opposing interpretations side by side.

## L10 — Mastery
- Skills: produce independent, structured, source-diverse assessments on demand.
- Theory: synthesis of all levels; knowing limits of prediction; recognizing propaganda and bias in sources (including one's own).
- Assessment: capstone — one full geopolitical assessment (country or crisis) defended in a live Q&A, plus a retrospective on 10 logged forecasts.
